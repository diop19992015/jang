## Question

Quelle phrase rapporte correctement « I am tired » (He said, “…") ?

- [ ] He said me that he is tired.
- [x] He said that he was tired.
- [ ] He said that he is tired.
- [ ] He told that he was tired.

> *Say* ne prend pas de complément de personne direct (pas ~~said me~~) et les temps se décalent : présent → **past simple** → *was tired*. *Tell* exigerait un complément : *He **told me** that…*.

## Question

Comment rapporte-t-on « Where do you live ? » posée par la professeure ?

- [ ] She asked me where do I live.
- [x] She asked me where I lived.
- [ ] She asked me where I did live.
- [ ] She asked me did I live where.

> En question indirecte, l’**ordre normal** s’impose (sujet + verbe), sans *do/does/did* ni inversion : *where I lived*, avec le décalage présent → passé.

## Question

Quelle phrase contient une faute ?

- [ ] According to the press, the news is reliable.
- [ ] The advertisement claims that the phone is the best.
- [x] According to the press, the informations are reliable.
- [ ] The journalist said that the story was false.

> *Information* est **indénombrable** : pas de pluriel ~~informations~~. On dira *The **information is** reliable* ou *The **pieces of information are** reliable*. Les trois autres phrases sont correctes.

## Question

Dans « A rumour spread quickly on social media », que signifie *spread* ?

- [ ] Étaler (physiquement)
- [x] Se répandre, se diffuser
- [ ] Payer
- [ ] Vérifier

> *To spread* = se répandre : c’est le verbe consacré aux rumeurs, aux nouvelles et aux maladies (*the news spread fast*). À ne pas confondre avec *to verify* (vérifier) ni *to share* (partager un post).
