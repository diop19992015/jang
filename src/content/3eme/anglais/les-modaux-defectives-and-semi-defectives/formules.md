## Les modaux défectifs : liste et emplois

| Modal | Emploi principal | Exemple | Traduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **can** | capacité, permission | *I **can** speak English.* | Je sais parler anglais. |
| **could** | capacité passée, permission polie, possibilité | ***Could** you help me?* | Pourriez-vous m’aider ? |
| **may** | permission polie, probabilité | *You **may** go now.* | Vous pouvez y aller maintenant. |
| **might** | probabilité faible | *It **might** rain.* | Il risque de pleuvoir. |
| **must** | obligation intérieure, certitude | *I **must** study.* | Je dois étudier. |
| **shall** | offre, suggestion (avec *I/we*) | ***Shall** we open the door?* | Ouvrons-nous la porte ? |
| **should** | conseil | *You **should** sleep early.* | Tu devrais dormir tôt. |
| **will** | volonté, futur | *I **will** help you.* | Je t’aiderai. |
| **would** | politesse, habitude passée | ***Would** you sit down?* | Vous voulez vous asseoir ? |
| **ought to** | conseil + moralité | *We **ought to** respect elders.* | Nous devrions respecter les aînés. |
| **had better** | conseil pressant | *You**’d better** run!* | Vaut mieux courir ! |

:::formule La construction d’un modal
$$
\text{modal} + \text{verbe nu (base verbale)}
$$
Exemples : *can swim*, *must go*, *should revise*, *may come*.
Jamais : ~~can to swim~~, ~~cans swim~~, ~~must going~~, ~~should to go~~.
:::

:::remarque Questions et négations
Les modaux se passent de *do/does/did* :

| Phrase | Question | Négation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *He **can** swim.* | ***Can** he swim?* | *He **cannot (can’t)** swim.* |
| *She **must** go.* | ***Must** she go?* | *She **must not (mustn’t)** go.* |
| *You **should** wait.* | ***Should** you wait?* | *You **should not (shouldn’t)** wait.* |
| *We **have to** leave.* | ***Do** we **have** to leave?* | *We **don’t have to** leave.* |
:::

## Obligation, interdiction, absence d’obligation

| Idée | Forme | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obligation (volonté) | **must** | *You **must** be quiet in class.* |
| Obligation (règle extérieure) | **have to** | *Pupils **have to** wear uniforms.* |
| Obligation passée | **had to** | *I **had to** take a bus yesterday.* |
| Interdiction | **mustn’t** | *You **mustn’t** cheat in the exam.* |
| Absence d’obligation | **don’t have to / needn’t** | *You **don’t have to** come on Saturday.* |
| Conseil | **should / ought to** | *You **should** revise every day.* |
| Conseil pressant | **had better** | *You**’d better** see a doctor.* |

## Capacité, permission, possibilité

| Idée | Forme | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacité — présent | **can** | *Aminata **can** ride a bike.* |
| Capacité — passé | **could** | *He **could** read at five.* |
| Capacité — futur / infinitif | **be able to** | *I **will be able to** swim next year.* |
| Permission — informelle | **can** | ***Can** I sit here?* |
| Permission — polie | **could / may** | ***May** I come in?* |
| Possibilité générale | **can** | *The sea **can** be rough in August.* |
| Probabilité | **may / might** | *He **may** be at the market.* |
| Certitude déduite | **must** | *She’s not at home; she **must** be at school.* |

## Les semi-modaux

:::formule Semi-modaux : formes complètes
| Semi-modal | Passé / négation | Exemple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **have to** | *had to* / *don’t have to* | *I **had to** wait.* |
| **ought to** | *ought not to* | *You **ought to** apologise.* |
| **used to** | *didn’t use to* | *We **used to** live in Thiès.* |
| **need** | *needn’t / don’t need to* | *You **needn’t** hurry.* |
| **dare** | *daren’t* | *He **daren’t** tell his father.* |
| **be able to** | *was/were able to* | *She **was able to** answer.* |
:::

:::remarque *Must* n’a pas de passé
Au passé, on utilise **had to** : *Yesterday I **had to** get up at 5 a.m.* À la question *Must he…?*, la réponse négative est *No, he **doesn’t have to*** (ou *He **needn’t***).
:::
