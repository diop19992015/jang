## Question

Comment rapporte-t-on : « I am revising for the BFEM, » he said ?

- [ ] He said he is revising for the BFEM.
- [x] He said (that) he was revising for the BFEM.
- [ ] He said he had revised for the BFEM.
- [ ] He told he was revising for the BFEM.

> Le verbe de parole est au passé → décalage : *am revising* (present continuous) devient **was revising** (past continuous), et *I* devient *he*. *Tell* exige une personne après lui : ~~*told he*~~ est fautif, il faudrait *told me*.

## Question

« Where do you live? » she asked. Quelle est la forme correcte ?

- [ ] She asked where did I live.
- [ ] She asked where do I live.
- [x] She asked where I lived.
- [ ] She asked where was I living.

> Dans une question rapportée, on garde le **mot interrogatif** (*where*) mais on remet l’**ordre direct** : sujet + verbe, sans inversion ni auxiliaire *do*. Le temps recule aussi : *live* → *lived*.

## Question

Comment rapporter : « Don’t be late, » the teacher said to us ?

- [ ] The teacher said us to not be late.
- [ ] The teacher asked us don’t be late.
- [x] The teacher told us not to be late.
- [ ] The teacher told us to not be late.

> Une impératif devient **told / asked + personne + to + infinitif** ; la négation se place **avant l’infinitif** : *not to be late*. Les propositions ~~*to not be*~~ et ~~*don’t be*~~ ne sont pas admises dans cette structure.

## Question

Il a demandé : « Are you ready? » → quelle phrase rapportée ?

- [ ] He asked was I ready.
- [ ] He asked if was I ready.
- [x] He asked if I was ready.
- [ ] He asked that I was ready.

> Une question **fermée** (oui/non) se rapporte avec **if** ou **whether**, suivis d’une phrase à l’**ordre direct** : *if I was ready* — plus d’inversion. Le décalage transforme *are* en *was*.
