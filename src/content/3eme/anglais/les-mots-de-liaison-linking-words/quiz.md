## Question

Complète : « We stayed at home ______ it was raining all day. »

- [ ] although
- [ ] but
- [x] because
- [ ] however

> La relation est une **cause** (pourquoi sommes-nous restés ?) et le mot est suivi d’une phrase (*it was raining*) : on choisit **because**. *Although* exprimerait la concession et *but* l’opposition.

## Question

Quelle phrase est correcte ?

- [ ] Although he was tired, but he continued working.
- [x] Although he was tired, he continued working.
- [ ] He was tired, however he continued working.
- [ ] In spite of he was tired, he continued.

> *Although* suffit à exprimer la concession : on ne lui ajoute jamais *but*. *However* doit ouvrir une phrase séparée avec une virgule, et *in spite of* doit être suivi d’un nom ou d’un *-ing*.

## Question

Complète : « You will fail ______ you work harder. »

- [x] unless
- [ ] because
- [ ] although
- [ ] moreover

> *Unless* = sauf si : « Tu échoueras à moins que tu ne travailles plus. » *Unless* remplace ici *if… not* : *You will fail **if you don’t** work harder.*

## Question

Quelle phrase exprime le mieux la conséquence ?

- [ ] He revised every evening. Moreover, he passed the exam.
- [x] He revised every evening, so he passed the exam.
- [ ] He revised every evening because he passed the exam.
- [ ] He revised every evening although he passed the exam.

> La conséquence s’exprime avec **so** (ou *therefore, as a result*) : cause → conséquence. *Moreover* ajouterait une idée nouvelle, *because* inverserait la cause, et *although* exprimerait une concession.
